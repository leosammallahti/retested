# Writing the sentence a player reads (2026-09-22)

The deck dealt FReD's `description` column as its claim. Most of those cannot be played:

> Main effect of condition on the applicability of the insanity defense
> Participants judged the man as more likely to be the pilot than the woman,
> Conjugate schedules are variations of continuous reinforcement schedules where the consequent events co-vary
> proportionally with a specific dimension of the response. The participants apply force to a dynamometer…

16% were under 60 characters and 6% over 250; one ends in a comma because the comma is in the data. The owner's
verdict was that they are not good enough. That is the **existing** deck — the finds this pipeline adds were
never the only problem.

`pipeline/claims.py` writes two sentences per card: what the study claimed, and how it was tested. The design is
[`docs/planning/designs/D3-game.md`](planning/designs/D3-game.md) §8, written before any of this existed.

**689 of 749 cards, real spend $0.23.** The figure this work had been carrying was ~$3.50 for the whole site.

| | old | new |
|---|---|---|
| claim | Main effect of condition on the applicability of the insanity defense | People endorse the insanity defense less when a crime is explained by the perpetrator's environment than by genetic factors. |
| method | — | 298 adults read a vignette about a criminal case in which the cause of the behavior was described as environmental, genetic, genetic plus impulse control, or genetic plus self-control, then rated how applicable the insanity defense was. |

## What the writer never sees

The replication's own verdict quote. It is the answer, and a model that has read it will shade the claim towards
it however firmly it is told not to. Only the original study's material goes in. For the famous tier that also
means the curated `critiques` and `replication_es` fields, which *are* the answer, and citation counts, because
how famous a study is predicts whether it replicated.

## Four prompt versions, because reading 24 cards myself was not enough

I read the whole pilot and found a tautology, two correct refusals and a false-positive leak flag. Four
reviewers each took one lens over the same 24 and the fidelity lens called **14 of 24 non-ok**. What they found:

- **Invented directions.** FReD's result sentences are frequently an omnibus F, χ² or MANOVA with no direction
  in them. Asked to write a finding from that, the model took the direction from the title. It now has to fill
  *"<who>, compared with <what>, showed <more / less / none> <of what>"* from the study's own words and set
  `insufficient` if it cannot. 8% of the deck refuses on that rule.
- **Manipulation checks as findings.** "People rate a source as more expert when the source is presented as
  having high expertise" — true by construction, and the leakage lens called it the purest leak in the deck.
- **Absences reversed.** "Goats do not follow the gaze of a human agent" came back as "Goats follow the gaze
  direction of a human". A rule against signalling null results reads like a rule against negative sentences.
  There is now an explicit rule, a `finding_is_absence` field, and a mechanical flag.
- **Uneven hedging.** "tend to", "depends partly on", "most" on some cards and not others. A writer hedges more
  when it doubts the finding, so the register is a leak channel of its own. Hedges are banned and flagged.
  I did **not** take the same reviewer's further proposal to ban causal verbs: "makes", "increases" reveal that
  the study was an experiment, but so does the method line printed underneath, so hiding the design in the
  claim's grammar buys nothing.
- **Formatting tells.** Sample sizes spelled out on some cards, digits on others, once appended as a separate
  sentence. Digits everywhere now, checked mechanically.
- **Outdated clinical language** carried forward from old abstracts ("a single profoundly retarded adult
  woman"). Described as a paper written today would.

## The root fix was not a prompt

The writer was being handed a bare result sentence because nobody had ever fetched the originals' abstracts.
OpenAlex serves them free and we already held the identifiers: `pipeline authors --deck` got **387 of 501** card
originals an abstract for **$0.001 and nine calls**, and most of the invention disappeared with it. The refusal
rate on the pilot's hardest cards went from 25% to 8% across the deck once the writer had something to read.

## The fairness test

D3 §7 specifies a model-guess test: if a model can predict the answer from the claim alone, the claims leak.
Four blind guessers on the 22-card pilot, balanced 11/11:

| | correct | |
|---|---|---|
| overall | 70/88 | 80% |
| where the guesser recognised the study | 31/36 | 86% |
| **where it read from the card alone** | **39/52** | **75%** (95% CI 62–85%) |

75% from the card alone is well above chance. The guessers were asked to explain it, and all four pointed at
*content*: sample size, how far the measure sits from the manipulation, and moderator claims. That is what a
player is supposed to reason from — this site's own headline numbers are exactly such patterns.

**But the explanation they offered does not survive checking.** Every guesser said failures cluster at small
samples. In the pilot the successful cards have a median N of 132 against 112 for the failures, and across the
whole deck 94 against 79 — the opposite direction, weakly. So the 75% is real and its cause is not the one the
guessers gave. The most likely remainder is recognition they did not flag. **It is not explained by the writing:
all four said the two groups are written alike, and the register lens agreed.**

This is a property of the deck, not a defect introduced by rewriting the claims, and the same test should be run
against the old descriptions before anyone concludes the rewrite made it worse.

## What the build refuses

The leak regex applies to the claim **and** the method line, which are both on the card, but not to the effect
label, which is the reveal screen's heading — "Productive Failure Learning" is the real name of that phenomenon.
Two cards dealing the identical claim with different answers now fails the build. The daily ten draws only from
the 689 written cards; endless still deals the 60 that kept their source description.
