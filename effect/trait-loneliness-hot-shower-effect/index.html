Trait loneliness hot shower effect

Bargh & Shalev, 2012

The claim

People who feel chronically lonelier take more warm baths and showers, as if physical warmth stands in for the missing social warmth.

In the original authors' words: “We expected that the cold temperature prime would increase participants’ scores on the loneliness scale, as experiences of physical coldness should increase feelings of social coldness (dis-connectedness)...Planned comparisons indicated that the cold-pack…”

Also filed as: Loneliness increases warm bathing

How the original study workedCompares existing traits or groupsmeasured as What people said or rated

What people did

People said how lonely they felt and how often they took warm baths or showers.

What the original found

The lonelier people were, the more warm baths and showers they took.

What happened when others tried it

5 attempts, oldest first
  1. 2014
    Found itShalev & Bargh

    What they did The authors ran further studies with people in India, Israel, and North America. They measured how lonely people were and how much physical warmth they got from bathing and showering. They also re-ran their analysis with the bathing frequency item removed from the warmth index.

    What they found In all three samples, loneliness was correlated with warmth from bathing and showering, and the overall effect was reliable across the three. It was smaller than in the authors' original studies. Removing the bathing frequency item did not substantially change the results. The authors respond to Donnellan et al., whose large-sample study found the relation between trait loneliness and warmth from bathing to be much smaller than in the authors' initial smaller samples. They add that in the 2 studies where Donnellan et al. most closely followed the original procedure, they did replicate the original results, but not in the other 7 studies, which had considerable procedural changes.

    Written by an AI model from the later paper’s abstract, and read against it by another AI model.

    “again showing significant correlations between loneliness and physical warmth extraction from bathing and showering”

  2. 2014
    Did not find itFerrell et al.

    What they did The study looked at the link between water temperature and trait loneliness, using participants from the University of Texas, Austin. It tested the correlation reported in Study 1a of the original paper, which was about how people showered.

    What they found The authors report that their results do not replicate the correlation between water temperature and trait loneliness reported in Study 1a of the original paper. They add that the results generally match those reported by Donnellan, Lucas, and Cesario.

    Written by an AI model from the authors’ own words, and read against it by another AI model.

  3. 2014
    Did not find itWortman et al.Archives of Scientific Psychology

    What they did Using procedures very similar to those in the original study and a sample more than five times larger, 260 people held either a hand warmer or a cold pack for 1 minute. Trait loneliness was then measured with a short form of the UCLA Loneliness Scale, and the Big Five personality traits were assessed.

    What they found Loneliness scores did not differ between the warm and cold groups, with an effect size of essentially zero. Excluding people who reported suspicion about the link between the packs and loneliness did not change this, and the packs had no effects on personality traits. This did not match the original finding that cold-pack holders reported more loneliness. The authors suggest further research is needed to determine whether there is a connection between physical warmth and interpersonal warmth.

    Written by an AI model from the later paper’s abstract, the authors’ own words and a reviewer’s note, and read against them by another AI model.

    “Our results showed that holding a hand warmer or cold pack for 1 min had no effect on trait loneliness in our study, with an effect size of essentially zero.”

  4. 2015
    Found part of itDonnellan et al.Emotion

    What they did Across nine studies, participants completed a loneliness questionnaire (the UCLA Loneliness Scale) and answered questions about their showering or bathing habits. Some studies used the team's own bathing measures; others used the original study's measures. The first study copied the original closely with a sample more than four times larger than the original's 51 college students.

    What they found Using the team's own measures, four studies totalling 1,153 people found no evidence that loneliness was linked to bathing habits. Five studies totalling 1,920 people using the original measures gave an overall result that was not statistically significant. In the first study, the link was trivial and in the opposite direction from the original. The authors say the results cast doubt on the idea of a strong connection between trait loneliness and personal bathing habits related to warmth.

    Written by an AI model from the later paper’s abstract, the authors’ own words and a reviewer’s note, and read against them by another AI model.

    “we found no evidence for an association between trait loneliness and a composite index of showering or bathing habits in a combined sample of 1,153 participants from four studies.”
