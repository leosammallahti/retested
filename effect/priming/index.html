Tested the original paper

With a Clean Conscience

Schnall et al. · 2008 · Psychological Science · doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02227.x

Study 1

People did a task meant to bring the idea of cleanliness to mind, while others did a neutral task. Then everyone rated how morally wrong certain actions were. People who had cleanliness on their minds rated the actions as less wrong than people who did the neutral task.

Study 2

People watched a disgusting film clip. Afterward, some washed their hands and others did not. Then everyone rated how wrong several moral wrongdoings were. On average, the people who washed their hands rated the wrongdoings as less wrong than the people who did not wash.

Does cleanliness influence moral judgments? Response effort moderates the effect of cleanliness priming on moral judgments

Huang · 2014 · Frontiers in Psychology · doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01276

Study 2

Adults online did a quick task that brought to mind either cleanliness or a neutral idea, and were told to respond fast without paying much attention. Then they rated how wrong the behaviors in several stories were. People who had cleanliness on their minds judged them as less wrong.

    What they found The authors judge the replication to be an informative failure to replicate the original finding.
