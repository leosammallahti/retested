Scarcity effect - Physical pain

Chou et al., 2016

The claim

Feeling financially insecure, such as being unemployed or recalling money worries, makes people feel more physical pain.

In the original authors' words: “Participants in the lack-of-control condition reported more than double the physical pain (M = 13.75, SD = 21.49) reported by participants in the complete-control condition (M = 6.57, SD = 11.62), t(98) = 2.04, p = .04, d = 0.41”

How the original study workedSmall cue that spills overmeasured as What people said or rated

What people did

Some people were unemployed, some were told their state had high unemployment, and some were asked to think about past and future economic insecurity. Others had none of these experiences. Everyone then reported how much physical pain they felt.

What the original found

People who had these experiences reported more pain.

A plain-words account of the experiment, written by an AI model and read against its paper before it was used.

Did it hold up?

How sure can we be?

what the record holds
  • 3 later attempts, reported in 3 papers, 2021–2023.
  • Author lists let us check 2 of 3 attempts: 2 were run by teams sharing no author with the original.
  • The coding sources disagree on 1 attempt; those are marked below.
  • Pooled across the attempts that report an effect size, the effect is r = .07 (95% range −.23 to .35), against a typical original of r = .20: 33% of the original’s size. (Here r is a scale from 0, no link, to 1, a perfect link.)
  • Each verdict is the replicating authors’ own conclusion, recorded by FORRT’s coders or checked by Retested reviewers against the later paper.

What happened when others tried it

3 attempts, oldest first
  1. 2021
    Found part of itO'Donnell et al.Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences

    What they did 603 people were asked to recall a period in their life, either one when they were economically insecure or one when they were economically secure. They then reported how much physical pain they felt.

    What they found People who recalled an economically insecure period reported more physical pain than people who recalled an economically secure period. The authors report this as one of 20 replications in their paper, which varied widely in how well they held up.

    Written by an AI model from the later paper’s abstract and the authors’ own words, and read against them by another AI model.

    “The results of an independent samples t-test indicated that participants who recalled an economically insecure period in their life reported more physical pain (M = 1.57, SD = 2.14) than participants who recalled an economically secure period (M = 0.78, SD = 1.49, t(601) = 5.24, p < .001, Cohen’s d = 0.43, 95% confidence interval (CI) = [0.27, 0.59].”
