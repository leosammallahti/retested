  1. Did not replicateWortman et al., 2014Archives of Scientific Psychology

    Can physical warmth (or coldness) predict trait loneliness? A replication of Bargh and Shalev (2012).

    What they did In a replication with procedures very similar to the original and a sample more than 5 times larger, a total of 260 people held either a hand warmer or a cold pack for 1 minute. The team assessed trait loneliness (the original measured it with a short form of the UCLA Loneliness Scale) and the Big Five personality traits.

    What they found People who held the cold pack did not report being lonelier than people who held the warm pack, and the effect size was essentially zero. Personality traits also did not differ between conditions. The original study had found that cold-pack holders reported higher loneliness, so this result was not the same. The effect also stayed nonsignificant after excluding people who reported suspicion about the link between the warmth-coldness manipulation and the loneliness measure. The authors suggest further research is needed to determine whether physical warmth and interpersonal warmth are connected.

  2. Did not replicateMcCarthy, 2014OSF Registries

    The Effect of "Heat-Priming" on Hostile Perceptions

    What they did The later team tested whether being exposed to heat-related words (a "heat prime") changes whether people see others as hostile, compared with cold-related words or neutral words. The measure was hostile perceptions.

    What they found They found no evidence that heat primes affected hostile perceptions relative to cold primes or to neutral primes. This differs from the original finding.

  3. Did not replicateMcCarthy, 2014

    Close replication attempts of the heat priming-hostile perception effect

    What they did In two close replications with independent samples (688 people in total), participants were exposed to heat-related, cold-related, or neutral (non-temperature-related) primes. They then read an ambiguously aggressive vignette and judged how hostile its protagonist was. Each sample was more than two and a half times larger than the original study's.

    What they found Neither sample showed any evidence that heat primes changed hostile perceptions compared with cold primes or neutral primes. The original had found the protagonist judged as more hostile after heat primes. A meta-analysis found the heat-versus-neutral difference was not significantly different from zero, and the author concludes that heat priming does not reliably affect hostile perceptions.

  6. Did not replicateKlein et al., 2018Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science

    Many Labs 2: Investigating Variation in Replicability Across Samples and Settings

    What they did Participants took part in a replication of a study on temperature priming and concern about global warming. This replication was one of 28 run across many samples in 36 countries and territories, and 4,204 people were tested on this particular question.

    What they found The authors report that the result is not consistent with the idea that temperature priming alters concern about global warming. The effect was small, much weaker than the original finding, and in the opposite direction.
