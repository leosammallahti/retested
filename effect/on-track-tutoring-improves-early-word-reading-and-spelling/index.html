On Track tutoring improves early word reading and spelling

Solheim et al., 2018

The claim

Extra reading lessons for first graders likely to struggle, with a teacher and computer practice, improve how well they read and spell words.

In the original authors' words: “The sentence reading task consisted of 10 items in which a written sentence was followed by four pictures. The child was asked to choose the picture that best corresponded to the meaning of the sentence. The correct stimuli were presented in a random order.…”

How the original study workedTraining, therapy or rewardmeasured as Speed, accuracy or memoryChildren and teensSchools

What people did

Thirteen Norwegian schools were randomly sorted into three groups. First graders who seemed likely to struggle with reading got a teacher-led program plus a computer exercise, either with or without features that adapt to the child. Children at the control schools did not get the program.

What the original found

The children in the program did better at reading words and spelling than the children without it.

In the original authors’ words: “The sentence reading task consisted of 10 items in which a written sentence was followed by four pictures. The child was asked to choose the picture that best corresponded to the meaning of the sentence. The correct stimuli were presented in a random order.”

  1. 2024
    Found itSolheim et al.Journal of Research on Educational Effectiveness

    What they did All 42 schools in two Norwegian school districts were randomly assigned for first grade either to use the On Track reading program for students at risk of reading difficulties or to a control condition. Local school authorities ran the program, and schools and teachers had greater say over which students received it and for how long.

    What they found The program significantly reduced the number of at-risk students by the end of first grade, with positive effects on word reading and spelling; the reading-comprehension effect was positive but not statistically significant in the full sample. Effect sizes were reduced compared to the original try-out.
