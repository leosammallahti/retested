Tempting fate increases expected bad luck

Risen & Gilovich, 2008

The claim

Actions that seem to tempt fate, like skipping assigned reading, make people expect the matching bad outcome, more so under mental load.

In the original authors' words: “Participants believed that students were more likely to be called on if they had not done the reading (M = 3.43, SD = 2.34) than if they had (M = 2.53, SD = 2.24), F(1, 116) = 4.60, p < .05, d = 0.39. [This indicates that] people believe that negative…”

Also filed as: Tempting-fate actions raise perceived chance of negative outcomes

How the original study workedChanges the task or its wordingmeasured as What people said or rated

What people did

Study 2

People read about a student in a class. In one version the student had done the assigned reading, and in the other the student had skipped it. Then people guessed how likely it was that the teacher would call on that student.

What the original found

They thought a student who skipped the reading was more likely to be called on than one who had done it.

In the original authors’ words: “Participants believed that students were more likely to be called on if they had not done the reading (M = 3.43, SD = 2.34) than if they had (M = 2.53, SD = 2.24), F(1, 116) = 4.60, p < .05, d = 0.39.”

Study 6

People imagined being in a class, either having done the assigned reading or not having done it. Some also had to hold something in mind at the same time, which keeps the mind busy. Everyone then said how likely it was that they would be called on.

What the original found

When the mind was not busy, people who imagined not having done the reading thought they were more likely to be called on.

In the original authors’ words: “The main effects were qualified by the predicted interaction, however, F(1,116) 4.15, p <.05.”

How sure can we be?

what the record holds
  • 11 later attempts, reported in 11 papers, 2013–2026.
  • Author lists let us check 3 of 11 attempts: 3 were run by teams sharing no author with the original.
  • The coding sources disagree on 1 attempt; those are marked below.
  • Pooled across the attempts that report an effect size, the effect is r = .09 (95% range .07 to .11), against a typical original of r = .19: 47% of the original’s size. (Here r is a scale from 0, no link, to 1, a perfect link.)
  • Each verdict is the replicating authors’ own conclusion, recorded by FORRT’s coders or checked by Retested reviewers against the later paper.

  5. 2020
    Did not find itMathur

    What they did Students at four universities (754 people in all) were tested, after sites were chosen for being similar to the original study's site. The protocol was developed together with the original authors. Participants judged whether tempting fate would bring bad outcomes, with and without cognitive load. Six more universities and a new online sample were also tested.

    What they found Results from the four similar universities did not support the interaction between tempting fate and cognitive load, or the main effect of tempting fate. Results from the six additional universities were comparable, and the online sample was not meaningfully different. Both the interaction and the main effect were part of the original finding, though the authors' post hoc analyses did not show the original's results to be strongly inconsistent with theirs.

    Written by an AI model from the later paper’s abstract, the authors’ own words and a reviewer’s note, and read against them by another AI model.

    “Results from these sites did not support the interaction or the main effect”

  6. 2020
    Did not find itMathur et al.Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science

    What they did 754 people at four universities chosen for similarity to the original study's site took part in a preregistered replication. It used a new protocol built with the original authors and focused mainly on whether the belief that tempting fate brings bad outcomes grows stronger under cognitive load. Six more universities and a new Mechanical Turk sample were also tested.

    What they found Results from the four similar sites did not support the interaction or the main effect, and were comparable to results from the six additional universities. The Mechanical Turk results were not meaningfully different. Neither the original finding nor its interaction with cognitive load was reproduced. Post hoc analyses did not find strong evidence that the original's estimates were inconsistent with the new ones.

    What they did Several labs repeated Study 6 of the original paper on tempting fate, once with the protocol from an earlier replication project (RP:P) and once with a revised protocol that had been peer reviewed by experts before any data were collected.

    What they found Under both the RP:P protocol and the revised protocol, the combined result was not significant in the same direction as the original. This differs from the original finding.
