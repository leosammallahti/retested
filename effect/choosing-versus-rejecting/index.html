Choosing versus Rejecting

Shafir, 1993

The claim

An option with both big strengths and big flaws gets picked more often, and also rejected more often, than a bland one.

In the original authors' words: “As predicted, the value of Пc + Пr for Parent B (64+55 = 119) is significantly greater than the 100 that we would expect if choosing and rejecting were complementary (z = 2.48, p < .02)”

Also filed as: Choosing versus rejecting

How the original study workedChanges the task or its wordingmeasured as What people actually did or chose

What people did

People read a custody case about two parents. Some were asked which parent should be awarded the child, and others which should be denied the child.

What the original found

The same parent was the more popular answer to both questions.

In the original authors’ words: “As predicted, the value of Пc + Пr for Parent B (64+55 = 119) is significantly greater than the 100 that we would expect if choosing and rejecting were complementary (z = 2.48, p < .02)”

  4. 2018
    Did not find itKlein et al.Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science

    What they did Participants, 7,901 across the samples tested, were asked about a custody case involving two parents. Some were asked which parent should be awarded the child, others which parent should be denied the child.

    What they found The result was small and in the direction opposite that of the original finding. The authors say it is incompatible with the idea that negative features are weighted more strongly when people reject options and positive features more strongly when they select options.

  5. 2021
    Did not find itChandrashekar et al.Judgment and Decision Making

    What they did In a preregistered, very close copy of the original study, 1,026 people took part. They faced eight decision scenarios, each with one option that had more good and bad features and one that had fewer. Some were asked to choose an option and others to reject one.

    What they found Across the eight scenarios the results were not consistent with the original: two showed similar effects, two showed opposite effects, and four showed no effects. The team found no consistent support for the original hypotheses. In an extension, they found support for a different explanation, the accentuation hypothesis.

    Written by an AI model from the later paper’s abstract and the authors’ own words, and read against them by another AI model.

    “In a preregistered very close replication of the original study (N = 1026), we found no consistent support for the hypotheses across the eight problems: two had similar effects, two had opposite effects, and four showed no effects (overall d = −0.01 [−0.06,0.03]).”

  7. 2024
    Found itShafir & CheekJudgment and Decision Making

    What they did Shafir and Cheek reviewed a 2021 attempt by Chandrashekar and colleagues, which reported a failure to replicate some of the problems, to repeat the 1993 choose-or-reject problems with the same attributes as the original. They found that several of those attributes had changed their valence in the three decades since. They wrote new versions of the problems with updated attributes.

    What they found The new versions with updated attributes fully replicate the original 1993 findings, the authors report. They conclude that 'very close replications' across time, when stimuli may have changed their meaning or valence, can be highly misleading.

    Written by an AI model from the later paper’s abstract and the authors’ own words, and read against them by another AI model.

Original
‘ Choosing versus rejecting: Why some options are both better and worse than others’, Shafir 1993; 8 experiments with between-subjects design (i.e., two between-subjects conditions), across 8 studies sample size ranged from 170 to 398. [citations=779 (GS, July 2022)].
