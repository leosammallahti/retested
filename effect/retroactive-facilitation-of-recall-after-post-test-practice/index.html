Retroactive facilitation of recall after post-test practice

Bem, 2011

The claim

Practicing a random set of words after a memory test makes you remember those same words better on the earlier test.

In the original authors' words: “retroactive facilitation of recall”

Also filed as: Future word practice purportedly improves earlier recall

How the original study workedChanges the task or its wordingmeasured as Speed, accuracy or memory

What people did

People saw two curtains on a screen and guessed which one hid a picture. Only after they guessed did the computer randomly pick which side the picture was on.

What the original found

When the hidden picture was erotic, people picked the right curtain more often than chance.

In the original authors’ words: “Participants correctly identified the future position of the erotic pictures significantly more frequently than the 50% hit rate expected by chance: 53.1%, t(99) = 2.51, p = .01, d = 0.25.”

A plain-words account of the experiment, written by an AI model and read against its paper before it was used.

Did it hold up?
Largely failed

Most later attempts did not find it.

Of 12 later attempts, 1 found it, 1 found part of it, 8 did not find it; 2 have no clear verdict yet.

  9. 2013
    Found part of itSubbotskyThe Open Psychology Journal

    What they did Across three experiments, the team repeated Bem's method for testing whether later events affect earlier recall, with minor modifications. In the first, the experimenter knew the conditions but participants did not. In the second, the control condition, which was meant to show no effect, was run before the experimental condition.

    What they found In the first experiment's experimental condition, Bem's results were replicated, but the control condition showed a significant effect that mirrored it. In the second, both conditions were symmetrical, not significantly different from zero. In the third, the experimental results reversed. The author concludes the results marginally support robustness of Bem's effect but not its interpretation as inverted causality. The author suggests that the effect Bem reported is one of a number of possible outcomes of his method, which occur due to a direct effect of the observer's mind on the random number generator.

    Written by an AI model from the later paper’s abstract, the authors’ own words and a reviewer’s note, and read against them by another AI model.

    “Altogether, the results marginally support robustness of Bem’s reported effect.”

  11. 2022
    Did not find itMuhmenthaler, Dubravac & MeierPsychology of Consciousness: Theory, Research, and Practice

    What they did In a free recall experiment run over the Internet, people saw a list of words, then recalled as many as they could. Afterward, a computer picked some of the words for them to practice. Another version ran in the usual forward order, with practice before recall, as a control. The team also ran two priming experiments in both backward and forward versions.

    What they found In the free recall experiment, people recalled the same number of practiced words (11.4 on average) and control words (11.5). The team reports no precognition effects in any of the three experiments, so they did not find the original result. Exploratory analyses found some significant effects, which the authors say need testing in preregistered, confirmatory studies.

    Written by an AI model from the later paper’s abstract, the authors’ own words and a reviewer’s note, and read against them by another AI model.

    “the participants recalled the same number of practiced words (M = 11.4, SD = 3.9) and control words (M = 11.5, SD = 3.9)”
