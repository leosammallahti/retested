The review queue

2,548 candidate records are not yet in Retested's counted record. An AI model read each abstract, guessed which original study it tests and whether it replicated. No person has completed the source review required to count these rows. 298 test an original that already has a page here.

This queue is not a list of papers certified missing from FORRT. It includes FORRT's own extractor records, possible alternate versions and unresolved matches. A candidate can be counted as absent only after the bounded comparison described on the coverage and overlap page.

They come from two searches, kept apart so you can weigh them differently.FORRT's own extractor contributes 317, taken from its public candidate file.Found by Retested contributes 2,231: we searched OpenAlex for psychology papers with “replication” in the title and for papers citing an original already in this wiki, then had DeepSeek read each abstract. Of those, 1,211 are psychology replications, 795 are replications in other behavioural sciences and 225 are registered protocols with no results yet; 715 are preprints rather than journal articles. 567 have been matched to a specific original, 217 of them to one with a page here.

Two things here are machine-guessed, and we have measured how often each is right rather than guessing. Is it a replication at all? 1,233 finds came from a strong title/abstract signal, where the screen was right 86% of the time against 70 hand-checked papers; 968 came from a weaker signal, right 50% of the time on 40 checked papers. The usual mistakes are a study replicating its own Study 1, a new theory test, or a thesis. Is it linked to the right original? Every matching method has been checked by reading its links one by one, 147 of them so far: picked from the paper’s own reference list 92% (11/12), author and year against this wiki, strong 100% (2/2), the same, medium 63% (5/8), author and year via OpenAlex, strong 100% (1/1), the same, medium 75% (3/4), scored against the papers it cites, strong 47% (8/17), the same, medium 71% (12/17), the same, weak 73% (51/70), author and year via OpenAlex, weak 0% (0/3), author and year against this wiki, weak 100% (1/1). The weak bands are not unchecked — they were checked and they fail, usually by matching a surname and a year onto a paper from another field entirely. Only the methods above 90% appear beside a verdict on an effect page; the rest wait here. Every row below says which method found its original, so you know how hard to look.

Three things about how each study was done are shown where we can establish them without reading the paper, because FORRT codes them for very few entries and they change how much a result is worth. Preregistered: 125 of the 423 finds hosted on OSF are registrations, which OSF states outright. Replication type: 192 say in their own words whether they ran a direct, close or conceptual replication — 74 are conceptual, where a failure is weak evidence against the original. Independence: 403 were run by a team sharing no author with the original and 103 by one that does, which in FORRT's own data is the difference between a 38% and a 70% success rate. A match on a common surname alone is not counted as overlap.

This is not filing. 107 of the candidates below would change the verdict on an effect page if accepted, across 103 findings — turning “replicated once” into “replicated”, or into “contested”. Each row says which, before you decide.

Check a candidate against its source, record a rationale and evidence location, then accept it, correct the outcome, reject it, or hold it as unsure. Decisions begin as local browser drafts. Export the versioned JSON review packet to preserve or transfer every decision; importing the packet validates candidate identity before merging it. Export accepted rows as CSV and submit them to FORRT, so supported records can enter the shared database. The CSV uses FLoRA's own column names in FLoRA's own order — including alt_identifier_r and url_r for the 715 finds that are preprints or OSF deposits rather than journal articles — followed by the review rationale, checked evidence, preregistration, author overlap, and matching provenance. A curator should not have to take any of it on trust.
