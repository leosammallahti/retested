Can you connect with me now? How the presence of mobile communication technology influences face-to-face conversation quality

Przybylski & Weinstein, 2012 · doi.org/10.1177/0265407512453827

revealed result

Related effect: Phone presence reduces conversation quality · FORRT atlas ↗

What people did

Two people sat down to have a face-to-face conversation. In some pairs a mobile phone was present, and in others there was no phone.

What the original found

Conversations with a phone present came out worse on closeness, connection, and conversation quality than conversations without one.

In the original authors’ words: “In both, we found evidence they can have negative effects on closeness, connection, and conversation quality.”

What happened when others tried it

4 attempts, oldest first
  1. 2018
    Did not find itCrowley et al.Communication Studies

    What they did The team ran a partial replication of the earlier study on cell phones and conversation. Pairs of participants had a conversation, with a cell phone either placed in view or not, and the team measured how the pairs rated relational quality, empathy and other conversation outcomes, and whether the conversation was meaningful.

    What they found Cell phone presence did not significantly influence relational quality or empathy, and meaningfulness did not change the effect of cell phone presence on conversation outcomes. The authors write that this failed to replicate the original findings. They point to changing norms in cell phone use and competing explanations for cell phone effects as possible reasons.

  3. 2021
    Did not find itLinares & SellierPLOS ONE

    What they did In two experiments with 356 participants in 136 groups, strangers who had never met were put together to have a conversation. A smartphone sat in the room for some groups and a notebook for others. Afterward, the participants did creative tasks, in groups in both experiments and alone in the first.

    What they found In both experiments, the team failed to replicate the original result on relationship quality. They also found no effect of a phone's presence on creativity. The authors discuss possible reasons for the differences from the original results. They conclude that an effect of the mere presence of a phone on relationship quality and creativity is at minimum harder to find than previously assumed, and that the research helps qualify the view that smartphones are harmful.
