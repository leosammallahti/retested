Young infants prefer prosocial to antisocial others

Related effect: Preference for prosocial agents · FORRT atlas ↗

What people did

Infants watched puppet shows in which one puppet helped a character open a box and another puppet kept it shut. Afterward each infant could reach for one of the two puppets. In a matched version, the same box actions were done to something that wasn't a living character.

What the original found

In the main version, most infants chose the puppet that helped open the box over the one that blocked it.

In the original authors’ words: “Infants in the Social condition significantly preferred the Opener over the Closer puppet (”

  2. 2015
    Did not find itSalvadori et al.PLOS ONE

    What they did Nine-month-old babies watched a live puppet show in which a puppet tried to open a box to get a toy inside. A second puppet helped it, and a third puppet hindered it. The babies were then shown the helper and the hinderer and encouraged to reach for one. This was done twice by different groups of experimenters: Study 1 followed the published method as closely as possible, and Study 2 made some changes to the stimuli and procedure.

    What they found Babies' preference for the helper did not differ significantly from chance: 62.5% in Study 1 and 50% in Study 2. In the original study, 75% of 9-month-olds chose the helper.

  6. 2026
    Did not find itRimol et al.Child Development

    What they did Infants aged 9 months took part in two experiments, one in Norway (24 infants) and one in Indonesia (30 infants), run in 2022 and 2023. They used the box opening/closing paradigm: puppets acted as a Helper or a Hinderer, and the researchers recorded which puppet each infant preferred.

    What they found In Norway 58% of infants preferred the helper, and in Indonesia 57% did; neither was significantly different from chance. The original study found 75% of 9-month-olds preferring the helper, so these samples did not show the original pattern. A meta-analysis of 15 experiments from seven labs, including these, found 58% chose the helper, with the largest effects in North American studies, nearly all from a single laboratory.

    “In two independent samples of 9-month-olds, 58% of 24 Norwegian and 57% of 30 Javanese infants preferred the helper, which was not significantly different from chance.”

    Corrected original from the machine-cited 2007 hill-climbing study to Hamlin and Wynn's 2011 box-opening experiment, first published online in 2010. Original study's 9-month social condition found 12 of 16 infants selected the Opener; two new 9-month samples in Norway and Indonesia individually showed no significant preference. The later meta-analysis across age groups and paradigms showed a smaller positive 58% preference, so the failed verdict applies to the two direct conceptual experiments, not that pooled estimate.
