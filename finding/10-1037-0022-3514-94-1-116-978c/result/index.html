A needs-based model of reconciliation: Satisfying the differential emotional needs of victim and perpetrator as a key to promoting reconciliation.

Shnabel & Nadler, 2008 · doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.94.1.116

Full source and attempt record

revealed result

FORRT atlas ↗

What people did

People took one of two roles in a pair: writers or judges. Some of them went through an experience meant to put them in the victim or perpetrator position, and others were in a comparison condition. Afterward, everyone's need for power and need for social acceptance was measured.

What the original found

Writers in the main condition had a higher need for power than writers in the comparison condition, but judges did not differ.

In the original authors’ words: “writers in the experimental condition had a higher need for power compared with writers in the control condition, F(1, 95)= 22.00, p< .001, eta(sq)= .32, whereas the corresponding difference for judges was not significant, F(1, 95) =0.03, ns, eta(sq)= 00.”

Study 4

People imagined being either the one who had wronged someone or the one who had been wronged. Then they got a message, either saying they were accepted as a decent person or saying they had power in the situation.

What the original found

When the message came after the wrong was done, perpetrators were more willing to reconcile after the acceptance message, and victims after the empowerment message.

In the original authors’ words: “In line with our predictions, although the Message × Role interaction was nonsignificant in the before condition, F(1, 90) = .00, ns, η² = .00, it was significant in the after condition, F(1, 90) = 6.98, p < .01, η² = .07.”

A plain-words account of the experiment, written by an AI model and read against its paper before it was used.

what the record holds
  • 8 later attempts, reported in 8 papers, 2013–2020.
  • Author lists let us check 2 of 8 attempts: 1 was run by a team sharing no author with the original, 1 shared authors.
  • Pooled across the attempts that report an effect size, the effect is r = .06 (95% range −.01 to .13), against a typical original of r = .28: 21% of the original’s size. (Here r is a scale from 0, no link, to 1, a perfect link.)

What happened when others tried it

8 attempts, oldest first
  1. 2013
    Found itSimanTov-Nachlieli & ShnabelPersonality and Social Psychology Bulletin

    What they did In the first experiment, participants played variations of the dictator game, a money-sharing game, that put them in the position of a victim, a perpetrator, both at once ("duals"), or neither (control). The team then measured their needs for empowerment and for acceptance, their willingness to reconcile after messages from their adversaries, and antisocial versus prosocial behavior.

    What they found Victims showed a heightened need for empowerment and perpetrators a heightened need for acceptance, as in the original findings. Duals showed heightened needs for both at once, but their need for empowerment, not acceptance, drove their behavior. Like victims, and unlike perpetrators, duals showed greater antisocial than prosocial behavior.

    Written by an AI model from the later paper’s abstract and the authors’ own words, and read against them by another AI model.

    “Study 1 supported our hypotheses regarding duals’ emotional needs and behaviors. Replicating and extending previous findings within the framework of the needs-based model, we found that whereas victims and perpetrators showed heightened need for either agency or positive moral identity, respectively, duals showed enhancement in both needs simultaneously.”

  6. 2020
    Found part of itEbersole et al.Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science

    What they did Several labs repeated one of the original reconciliation studies. Each lab ran it twice: once with the protocol from the earlier Reproducibility Project: Psychology replication, and once with a revised protocol that had been peer reviewed by experts before any data were collected.

    What they found The combined result from the labs using the earlier protocol included zero effect. The combined result using the revised protocol was significant in the same direction as the original study. Across the paper's 10 tested studies, the revised and earlier protocols produced similar effect sizes.
