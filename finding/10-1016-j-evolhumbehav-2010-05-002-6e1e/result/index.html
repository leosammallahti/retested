  1. Did not replicateEngelbrecht et al., 2024Psychological Reports

    Fertility Fails to Predict Voter Preference for the 2020 Election: A Pre-Registered Replication of Navarrete et al. (2010)

    What they did The team ran a pre-registered conceptual retest, changing the voter-preference questions to cover the 2020 election instead of the 2008 one. Obama stayed in as a candidate. They asked women about voter preference in several matchups, including Obama against Donald Trump, and compared preference with each woman's conception risk across the reproductive cycle.

    What they found Contrary to the original findings, conception risk did not predict greater voter support for Obama as a younger, more attractive alternative to Trump. It was not a significant factor in the other matchups either. Candidate intelligence and participants' Dark Triad psychopathy scores were found to be factors in preference for Obama/Biden or Trump, respectively. The authors suggest that further research is needed that takes political factors into account.

    Written by an AI model from the later paper’s abstract, the authors’ own words and a reviewer’s note, and read against them by another AI model.

    “Contrary to the original findings, conception risk did not predict greater voter support for Obama as a younger, more attractive alternative to Donald J. Trump, nor was conception risk a significant factor in other matchups we presented to participants.”

  2. Did not replicateEdlund et al., 2025

    SCORE new data replication of Navarrete et al. (2010)

    What they did The team tested whether a woman's risk of conceiving relates to which candidate she says she prefers in a vote. They had 545 people take part and scored voting preference with the same coding scheme Navarrete and colleagues used.

    What they found The team reports that the original effect did not replicate, since conception risk was not significantly related to voting preference in one analysis. In the larger analysis, the relationship was significant but ran in the opposite direction.

    Written by an AI model from the authors’ own words, and read against it by another AI model.

    “The H effect did not replicate as there was no significant relationship between conception risk and voting preference using the coding scheme used by Navarrete et al, r(226) = -.086, p = .195. The H effect did not replicate as there was significant relationship in the opposite direction between conception risk and voting preference using the coding scheme used by Navarrete et al, r(545) = -.112, p = .009”
