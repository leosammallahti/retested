Can playing the computer game "Tetris" reduce the build-up of flashbacks for trauma? A proposal from cognitive science

Holmes, 2009 · doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0004153

Full source and attempt record

revealed result

Related effect: Tetris after a trauma film reduces later intrusions

What people did

People watched a film with scenes of real injury and death, then had a 30-minute break. After that, some played the computer game Tetris for 10 minutes and others did nothing. For the next week, their unwanted memory flashbacks were tracked.

What the original found

The Tetris group had fewer flashbacks than the do-nothing group.

In the original authors’ words: “Results indicated that compared to the no-task condition, the "Tetris" condition produced a significant reduction in flashback frequency over 1-week.”

A plain-words account of the experiment, written by an AI model and read against its paper before it was used.

  2. 2020
    Found itBadawiClinical Psychological Science

    What they did In an independent, preregistered replication of findings that primarily originated from a single research group, 110 participants watched a trauma film and were randomly assigned to one of three groups. One group did a task that included playing Tetris, one did a different visuospatial task (D-Corsi), and one was a control group. Participants then reported intrusive memories of the film over the following week.

    What they found The group whose task included Tetris reported fewer intrusive memories of the film than both the D-Corsi group and the control group. This matches the original finding that playing Tetris after a trauma film reduced intrusive memories.

  4. 2026
    Did not find itAbrams et al.Memory & Cognition

    What they did Participants watched a film showing traumatic scenes, either on a standard monitor or in virtual reality. Afterward they played Tetris for 0, 7 or 14 minutes. The team counted the intrusive memories of the film that people reported across the next 5 days.

    What they found Unexpectedly, people who played Tetris for 14 minutes reported more intrusive memories across the 5 days than people who played for 0 minutes. The original study found fewer flashbacks over one week after Tetris, so this result went the opposite way, though the new film format, timing and follow-up make this a looser test than a direct repeat.
