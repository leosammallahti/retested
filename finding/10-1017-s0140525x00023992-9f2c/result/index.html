What people did

Men and women in 37 groups of people, drawn from 33 countries on six continents and five islands, answered questions about what they value in a potential mate. The researchers compared what the women valued with what the men valued.

What the original found

Women valued signs of a partner's ability to gain resources more highly than men did.

In the original authors’ words: “Females were found to value cues to resource acquisition in potential mates more highly than males. Characteristics signaling reproductive capacity were valued more by males than by females.”

What happened when others tried it

  1. 2002
    Found itStasioDissertation, Louisiana State University

    What they did The researcher ran one study to repeat earlier findings on what people want in a mate. The study used a paired comparison method to build scales of mate preferences, and also recorded reaction times while people made their decisions.

    What they found Paired comparison scaling generally replicated gender differences in mate preferences consistent with evolutionary predictions, as in the original. Reaction time suggested an underlying psychological continuum of selection criteria.

  3. 2020
    Found itConroy-BeamPsychological Science

    What they did The team surveyed 14,399 people across 45 countries about the kinds of partners they prefer, in a preregistered attempt to repeat classic studies of mate preferences. They also compared preferences across countries.

    What they found Men, more than women, preferred attractive, young mates, and women, more than men, preferred older mates with financial prospects. The authors say support for these universal sex differences remains robust. In countries with more gender equality, both sexes had partners closer to their own age, but beyond partner age, neither disease prevalence nor gender equality robustly predicted sex differences or preferences across countries.

    Buss 1989 DOI 10.1017/S0140525X00023992 used 37 cultural samples, total N=10,047, and reported that women valued resource-acquisition cues in potential mates more than men. Walter et al. 2020 explicitly identifies Buss 1989 as a classic 37-culture target and uses a newly collected 45-country sample N=14,399; its preregistered analysis supports women rating ideal mates’ financial prospects more highly. This row is restricted to that sex difference, not country-level gender-equality/pathogen predictions, which the later paper did not robustly support.
